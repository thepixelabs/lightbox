01 Getting in

There is nothing to import into.

Most editors want a library before they will let you work. Lightbox skips that step on purpose. Drag a file, a selection, or a folder onto the window and the editor opens on it, with a filmstrip of whatever you brought. Nothing is copied, nothing is indexed, and your photographs stay exactly where you left them on disk.

DSCF2371.RAF
  1. DropFiles, a selection, or a folder. There is an ordinary open dialog too, and a folder browser if you would rather navigate to things. A Dropbox or Drive folder is just a folder, so those work today.
  2. EditThe picture is on screen straight away and swaps to the full render a moment later. Nothing waits behind a progress bar.
  3. Come back laterLightbox recognises a photograph by its contents, not by its filename. Rename it, move it to another drive, open it again next year, and your edit is still on it.

02 The develop rail

Colour is the whole product.

Lightbox is not trying to be an everything app. It is trying to be very good at turning a raw capture into the picture you saw when you pressed the shutter. Every control runs on your own GPU in floating point, and the picture keeps up with the slider.

Drag a point

Tone curve

A parametric curve for broad shape and a point curve for precision, on RGB or any single channel. Values are evaluated in the graph rather than baked, so a curve you draw today renders identically on a machine you have not bought yet.

HSL and colour grading

Eight hue bands with hue, saturation and luminance each, worked in OkLCh so a saturation push does not drag the hue with it. Three grading wheels sit on top for shadows, midtones and highlights.

White balance that solves

Temperature and tint are solved against the camera's own colour matrix rather than nudged as raw multipliers, and the eyedropper neutralises from any pixel you click.

Tone and presence

Clarity and texture are guided-filter local contrast with halo guards rather than a blunt unsharp mask. Dehaze uses a dark-channel prior. The black and white mixer weights by hue band and can propose its own mix.

Crop and straighten

Eight handles, aspect locks, flips, and a straighten angle that constrains the frame to stay inside the image while you rotate it.

Histogram on the GPU

Computed by the same pass that renders the frame and read back without stalling it, with a clipping overlay you can toggle to see exactly which pixels you have thrown away.

Export

JPEG, PNG or TIFF, at eight or sixteen bits, resized on the long edge, converted to sRGB, Display P3 or Adobe RGB, with output sharpening. One image or the whole set.

Why it keeps up. The interface and the render engine share one graphics device, so a finished frame never has to travel back through the processor to reach your screen. Move one slider and only the part of the picture that depends on it is worked out again.

Everything here is global. Masking and local adjustments are not built yet.

03 Looks

Thirty-six looks, and every one of them opens.

A look in Lightbox is not a filter laid over your photograph. It is a set of slider positions. Apply one and every control moves to where that look put it, so you can see how it was built and carry on editing from there. Drag anything below and the picture answers immediately.

The photograph is a real raw file and the looks are the real shipped values, but the grading here runs in your browser. The application does the same arithmetic on your GPU, at full resolution, in floating point.

04 Your work

There is no save button.

Every move you make is written down the moment you make it. Pull the power cord in the middle of a session and the picture comes back exactly where you left it. That is not something we are asking you to take on trust: the test suite kills the program mid-edit, on purpose, over and over, and checks that nothing was lost.

  • Undo as far back as you like, and it is still there tomorrow
  • Named snapshots, so you can keep two versions of a picture and jump between them
  • Your original file is opened read-only and never written to
  • XMP sidecars next to the photograph, if you want other software to read your edits

Your edits are values, not baked pixels. They sit in a small file on your own disk, and they can be written alongside the photograph as a sidecar. Nothing is held on a server, nothing expires, and there is no subscription that can switch the program off. The source is public, so the format and the arithmetic are both there to read.

Lightbox does not send anything anywhere today. When it starts reporting anonymous usage figures, so we can see which controls people actually reach for, you will be asked on the first run and you will be able to say no. It will never include your files, your filenames, or anything from inside your photographs.

05 Honest status

Where the project actually is.

Lightbox is pre-1.0 and moving. Below is the whole picture: what you get if you build it today, what does not exist yet, and where it has and has not actually been run. Nothing in the middle column is described anywhere else on this page as though it works.

Working today

  • Drag and drop, open dialog and folder browsing
  • Raw files from Canon, Nikon, Sony, Fujifilm, Olympus, Pentax, Panasonic and DNG CR2 CR3 NEF ARW RAF ORF PEF RW2 DNG
  • JPEG, PNG and TIFF through the same pipeline
  • White balance, exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, whites and blacks
  • Parametric and point tone curves, per channel
  • HSL, vibrance, saturation and three-way colour grading
  • Clarity, texture, dehaze and a black and white mixer
  • Crop, straighten and flips with an interactive gizmo
  • Creative LUTs from .cube and HaldCLUT files
  • 36 bundled presets, plus your own, with XMP import and export
  • History, snapshots and crash-safe persistence
  • Export to JPEG, PNG and TIFF with resize, depth, colour space and sharpening
  • Dockable panels, full theming, remappable keyboard, preferences
  • A headless CLI that drives the whole pipeline

Not built yet

  • Masking and local adjustments
  • Healing, cloning and removing things
  • Noise reduction and sharpening
  • Lens corrections and perspective
  • Any AI at all
  • A demosaic of our own
  • Camera colour profiles
  • A before and after view
  • Watermarking and metadata on export
  • Signing in to cloud storage from inside Lightbox

Masking and local adjustments are the next thing we want to build, and there is no date on them. When AI arrives it will run on your own machine and hand you sliders you can move, not a finished picture you cannot take apart. Cloud storage would point at your storage account, not ours.

Before you build it

  • There are no installers. You build Lightbox from source, and you will need Rust on your machine to do it.
  • Full raw decoding is a build flag. With it on, Lightbox reads the real sensor data. With it off, raw files still open, but through the small JPEG the camera tucked inside them, which is not enough to push white balance or pull a highlight back. Turning it on means installing LibRaw first.
  • macOS on Apple Silicon is the only platform anyone has really used. Every measurement and every crash drill in the repository was done there.
  • Windows and Linux compile and pass the entire test suite on every commit, and nobody has yet sat down and edited a photograph on either one. Treat them as untested rather than supported.
  • No camera profiles ship yet, so colour comes from the matrix your camera wrote into the file. That is a decent starting point and it is not a calibrated profile. Building real ones needs physical colour targets, and we have not done it.

06 Get it

Four commands and a coffee.

There are no prebuilt binaries yet, so you build it yourself. The compiler version is pinned in the repository, which means rustup fetches the right one for you the first time you run this.

Clone and build
git clone https://github.com/thepixelabs/lightbox
cd lightbox
cargo xtask fixtures
cargo build --release --workspace
cargo run -p lightbox-shell
Turn on full raw decoding
# macOS. Elsewhere, install LibRaw and set LIBRAW_DIR.
brew install libraw
cargo build --release -p lightbox-rawproxy --features libraw
Make a real macOS app
cargo xtask bundle-mac --install
# builds Lightbox.app and puts it in /Applications
Drive it from a script
lightbox-cli open ~/Pictures/shoot --recursive
lightbox-cli edit set --image 1 exposure=+0.35 temp=5600
lightbox-cli export --image 1 --out out.jpg \
    --format jpeg --quality 92 --long-edge 2048

07 Contributing

Pull requests are welcome.

Every change has to pass five gates: it builds, the tests pass, clippy is clean with warnings denied, the formatting matches, and the dependency licences check out. That is the whole bar. Read CONTRIBUTING.md before you start on anything large, because the project keeps written specs and a log of where the code drifted from them, and matching that habit makes a change much easier to take.
