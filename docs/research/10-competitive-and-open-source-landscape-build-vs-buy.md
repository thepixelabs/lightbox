# Competitive and open-source landscape (build-vs-buy survey for every Lightbox subsystem)

The Lightroom-alternative field splits into three camps. Commercial rivals (Capture One, DxO PhotoLab 9, ON1 Photo RAW 2026, Luminar Neo, Apple Photos) each win on one axis — Capture One on tethering and color tooling, DxO on lab-measured lens corrections and its DeepPRIME XD3 neural denoiser, ON1/Luminar on bundled AI effects, Apple on on-device ML search — but none pairs a first-class DAM with a first-class raw engine the way Lightroom does. Open-source raw developers (darktable 5.6, RawTherapee 5.12, ART 1.26, Ansel, vkdt 1.0) collectively match or beat Adobe on raw rendering quality (RawTherapee's demosaicers, darktable's scene-referred filmic/sigmoid pipeline) but universally lose on culling speed, catalog UX, ML-assisted masking/search, and polish. Open-source DAMs (digiKam, PhotoPrism, Immich) show that faces, geotagging, and semantic search are solved problems with permissive-or-AGPL reference implementations, but none integrates a non-destructive raw editor. The clearest architectural lesson comes from vkdt (Johannes Hanika's Vulkan rewrite of darktable, v1.0 Dec 2025, BSD-2/GPL-3 dual): a GPU-resident node-graph DAG delivers order-of-magnitude interactivity gains over darktable's CPU/OpenCL fixed-order pixelpipe, and Ansel's 1.6-1.8x pipeline-overhaul speedups confirm how much headroom darktable's architecture leaves on the table.

On build-vs-buy, nearly every subsystem has a mature building block, but licensing dictates the assembly. A permissively-licensed Lightbox can safely link MIT/BSD/Apache cores (LCMS2, OpenColorIO, OpenImageIO, Halide, wgpu, ONNX Runtime, SQLite, libjxl/libavif, Adobe XMP Toolkit, rawler) and dynamically link LGPL libraries (LibRaw, rawspeed, libvips, lensfun, libgphoto2, libheif, Qt). The GPL landmines are exiv2 (GPL-2, so metadata should go through the BSD XMP SDK, Rust crates, or an ExifTool subprocess), the lensfun database (CC-BY-SA — fine to ship with attribution/share-alike on the data only), and — critically — the best demosaic and local-editing implementations (AMaZE, RCD, LMMSE, Markesteijn X-Trans) which live in GPL-3 codebases; these must be reimplemented from the published papers or taken from LibRaw's LGPL tier. ML is the cheapest differentiation to acquire: ONNX Runtime plus openly licensed models (Segment Anything, NAFNet/SCUNet, Real-ESRGAN, U2-Net, YuNet/SFace, CLIP) covers subject masking, denoise, upscaling, background removal, face clustering, and semantic search — the exact features that separate 2026 Lightroom from every FOSS competitor. Recommended v1 posture: Rust-or-C++ core, wgpu-based GPU node graph inspired by vkdt, LibRaw/rawler decode, SQLite catalog with Lightroom-compatible XMP sidecars, Qt or Tauri shell, ONNX-powered masking/search, and a strict no-GPL-in-process linking policy.

## Feature inventory

| Feature | Priority | Complexity | Description | Building blocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **darktable survey (primary FOSS benchmark)** | must | high | GPL-3.0 C/GTK3 app with Lua scripting and OpenCL acceleration; its fixed-order 'pixelpipe' runs ~90 modules with per-module drawn/parametric masks and a scene-referred workflow (filmic rgb, sigmoid) that is the best-documented FOSS tone-mapping design. Weaknesses to avoid: sluggish culling, intimidating UX, no ML masking, CPU-bound preview loop that the 5.6 release (June 2026) is still patching around; being GPL, its code cannot be copied into a permissive app but its module taxonomy, XMP sidecar schema, and darktable-chart/noise-profiling methodology are freely learnable. | darktable source (GPL-3, study only), rawspeed, darktable XMP schema docs, docs.darktable.org pixelpipe manual |
| **vkdt survey (architecture to emulate)** | must | very-high | Hanika's ground-up rewrite (v1.0, Dec 2025) processes everything as a Vulkan/GLSL node-graph DAG with multiple inputs/outputs and feedback edges, giving real-time raw stills and even raw video, plus U-Net denoising on-GPU. Dual BSD-2-Clause/GPL-3 licensing means portions are legitimately reusable in a permissive app, and its graph design is the single strongest architectural reference for Lightbox's engine. | vkdt (BSD-2/GPL-3 dual), Vulkan, GLSL compute shaders, GLFW |
| **RawTherapee survey (raw-quality ceiling)** | must | high | GPL-3 C++/GTKmm processor whose demosaicers (AMaZE, RCD, LMMSE, DCB, VNG4, Markesteijn for X-Trans), capture sharpening, and wavelet tooling define the open-source image-quality ceiling; 5.12 (2025) added further tools. Its weaknesses — no catalog, dated UI, overwhelming parameter count — mark exactly where Lightbox should differ; its algorithms must be reimplemented from papers, not copied, due to GPL. | RawTherapee source (GPL-3, study only), AMaZE/RCD/LMMSE published papers, RawPedia documentation |
| **ART survey (UX simplification of RawTherapee)** | should | medium | Alberto Griggio's GPL-3 fork (1.26.6, June 2026) that pruned RawTherapee to essential tools and added what users actually wanted: drawn+parametric local-edit masks, permanent snapshots stored in processing profiles, automatic perspective correction, CTL scripting, and proper XMP sidecar support. It is the best case study in curating a raw editor's surface area for a v1 product. | ART source (GPL-3, study only), CTL (Color Transformation Language) |
| **Ansel survey (darktable performance fork)** | could | medium | Aurélien Pierre's GPL-3 fork of darktable 4.0 that deleted ~30k lines and overhauled the pipeline for roughly 1.6-1.8x per-module speedups (claimed 4-100x faster preview refresh), proving darktable's architecture — not its algorithms — is the bottleneck. Single-maintainer bus factor makes it a lesson source, not a foundation. | Ansel source (GPL-3, study only), ansel.photos pipeline-overhaul writeups |
| **Capture One survey (pro tethering and color)** | should | high | Commercial C++/Qt-based flagship whose moats are studio tethering (fastest live capture-to-screen), a per-hue color editor, layer-based local adjustments, and the sessions-vs-catalog dual workflow that studio shooters prefer. Its subscription pivot created churn that Lightbox can court; feature-wise it defines the tethering and color-wheel bar for a pro tool. | libgphoto2 (for equivalent tethering), study only — proprietary |
| **DxO PhotoLab survey (denoise and optics moat)** | should | very-high | PhotoLab 9's DeepPRIME XD3 neural demosaic+denoise (now covering Bayer and X-Trans) and lab-measured per-lens/body optical modules are the industry's image-quality benchmark, with 9.x adding AI masks with feathering and high-fidelity compressed DNG output. Its weak DAM shows a pure-quality play is viable; open models (NAFNet, SCUNet) plus lensfun are the FOSS路 counterparts to chase. | ONNX Runtime, NAFNet (MIT), SCUNet, lensfun |
| **ON1 Photo RAW survey (all-in-one perpetual license)** | could | medium | Perpetual-license all-in-one whose 2026 release leans on creative effects (Depth Lighting, Split Field, Double Exposure, ICM motion), film-negative inversion, and applying filters directly to AI-selected regions like sky or architecture. Historically criticized for performance and catalog reliability — evidence that breadth without engine discipline hurts trust. | study only — proprietary |
| **Luminar Neo survey (AI-first consumer editor)** | could | medium | Skylum's AI-led editor (sky replacement with relighting, portrait enhancement, generative erase, distance-based exposure) that trades DAM depth and file-format rigor for one-click wow features. It demonstrates which ML features have mass appeal and how fragile catalogs destroy loyalty among serious users. | study only — proprietary; open analogues: SAM, LaMa inpainting (Apache-2.0) |
| **Apple Photos survey (platform ML baseline)** | could | medium | Free platform DAM with best-in-class on-device semantic search, face clustering, and sync, but shallow raw controls and no pro masking. It sets user expectations for zero-setup ML search that Lightbox must meet via CLIP-style embeddings, and its CoreML deployment pattern is the model for shipping ML on macOS. | CoreML, CLIP (MIT), study only — proprietary |
| **digiKam survey (strongest FOSS DAM)** | must | high | GPL-2+ Qt/C++ manager with the deepest open-source DAM feature set: hierarchical tags, ratings/labels, face detection+recognition (OpenCV YuNet/SFace), reverse-geocoded maps, powerful search, batch queue manager, and SQLite or MySQL backends. Users report it strains on very large archives and its bundled editor is weak — validating Lightbox's integrated-editor thesis; its GPL code is reference-only but its OpenCV model choices are directly reusable. | OpenCV (Apache-2.0), YuNet face detector, SFace embeddings, Marble/OSM mapping |
| **LightZone survey (permissive tone-science donor)** | could | low | Java-based editor open-sourced under BSD-3 whose Zone System tone mapper and ZoneMapper UI remain a unique interaction model for luminosity editing. Development is near-dormant, but its permissive license makes it one of the few full editors whose code and concepts can be lifted legally into a permissive Lightbox. | LightZone source (BSD-3, reusable) |
| **PhotoPrism and Immich survey (web/self-hosted DAM lessons)** | could | high | AGPL-3 self-hosted photo services — PhotoPrism (Go + TensorFlow classification) and the faster-moving Immich (TypeScript/ML with faces, CLIP search, maps, timeline, mobile auto-backup) — that define modern expectations for sync, mobile companion apps, and ML search. AGPL bars code reuse in a permissive app, but their model choices (CLIP, facial pipelines) and API/sync designs are directly instructive for a future Lightbox sync service. | CLIP (MIT), study only — AGPL-3 |
| **Raw decoding via LibRaw** | must | medium | The de-facto standard raw decoder (LGPL-2.1/CDDL dual) covering 1000+ cameras with metadata, thumbnails, and a set of demosaicers; dynamic linking keeps a permissive app clean. Broadest coverage per engineering hour of any option and the pragmatic v1 decode path. | LibRaw (LGPL-2.1/CDDL), libraw-rs bindings |
| **Raw decoding via rawspeed** | could | medium | darktable's decode library (LGPL-2.1) — faster than LibRaw on lossless-JPEG formats like CR2 and driven by a maintained per-camera XML database (crop, black levels, color matrices) that is itself a valuable dataset. Good second engine or benchmark reference; dynamic linking required for license hygiene. | rawspeed (LGPL-2.1), cameras.xml database |
| **Raw decoding via rawler (Rust)** | should | medium | MIT-licensed Rust library from the dnglab project that uniquely handles both pixel decoding and metadata parsing in one crate, plus DNG writing. Smaller camera coverage than LibRaw but the only permissive, memory-safe option — ideal core-engine choice with LibRaw as fallback for exotic bodies. | rawler (MIT), dnglab (DNG conversion) |
| **Demosaic algorithm suite (clean-room)** | must | very-high | State-of-the-art demosaicers (AMaZE, RCD, LMMSE, DCB, Markesteijn 1/3-pass X-Trans) exist only as GPL-3 implementations in RawTherapee/darktable, so a permissive Lightbox must reimplement from the published papers or settle for LibRaw's LGPL-tier AHD/DCB/PPG. This is the highest-value clean-room engineering item in the whole engine; GPU (compute-shader) ports also enable real-time preview. | Hirakawa AHD paper, RCD paper (Luis Sanz Rodríguez), LMMSE/DCB papers, LibRaw built-ins (LGPL) |
| **Lens correction via lensfun** | must | medium | The only open lens-profile system: LGPL-3 library plus a community database (CC-BY-SA) of distortion, vignetting, and TCA models for thousands of lenses, used by darktable and RawTherapee. Development is slow and modern mirrorless coverage is patchy, so Lightbox should also read the maker-embedded correction opcodes in raw/DNG files as DxO-class fallback. | lensfun (LGPL-3 code, CC-BY-SA data), DNG opcode lists, embedded lens-correction metadata |
| **ICC color management via LCMS2** | must | low | MIT-licensed Little CMS 2 handles ICC profile transforms, display profiling, and soft-proofing — table stakes for a pro tool and essentially free to integrate. Every FOSS competitor already uses it; no viable alternative needed. | Little CMS 2 (MIT) |
| **Scene-referred pipeline via OpenColorIO** | should | medium | BSD-3 OpenColorIO provides ACES-grade scene-referred color configs, view transforms, and GPU shader generation, letting Lightbox adopt the film-industry color architecture that darktable had to hand-roll. Pairs with LCMS2 (OCIO for working/view transforms, ICC for device I/O). | OpenColorIO (BSD-3), ACES configs |
| **Camera color profiling toolchain** | should | high | Accurate camera-to-working-space color needs per-body input profiles; Adobe's DNG SDK (permissive Adobe license) defines the DCP format, dcamprof (GPL-3, use as external CLI tool) builds profiles from ColorChecker shots, and darktable/RawTherapee ship matrices that are GPL-encumbered. V1 can ship Adobe-standard DCP reading plus rawler/rawspeed color matrices. | Adobe DNG SDK, dcamprof (GPL-3, subprocess only), Argyll CMS (AGPL, subprocess only) |
| **Image I/O via OpenImageIO and codec libraries** | must | low | Apache-2.0 OpenImageIO reads/writes TIFF, EXR, JPEG, PNG and more behind one API, while libjxl (BSD-3), libavif (BSD-2), and libheif (LGPL, dynamic-link) cover the modern export formats Lightroom now supports including HEIC import. Low-risk assembly work. | OpenImageIO (Apache-2.0), libjxl (BSD-3), libavif (BSD-2), libheif (LGPL-3), mozjpeg |
| **Fast thumbnailing via libvips** | should | low | LGPL-2.1 libvips is the fastest general-purpose imaging library for the grid/culling path — streaming, low-memory, multithreaded resizes ideal for generating preview pyramids for 100k+ image catalogs without touching the GPU engine. Dynamic-link keeps licensing clean. | libvips (LGPL-2.1) |
| **Kernel generation via Halide** | could | high | MIT-licensed Halide separates image-processing algorithms from CPU/GPU schedules, producing near-hand-tuned SIMD and GPU kernels from one source — a credible alternative to hand-writing every demosaic/blur/local-laplacian kernel twice for CPU and GPU. Adds a build-toolchain learning curve. | Halide (MIT) |
| **GPU abstraction via wgpu (or native Vulkan/Metal)** | must | high | wgpu (MIT/Apache-2.0, Rust) targets Vulkan, Metal, and DX12 from one WebGPU-style API with WGSL compute shaders — the modern cross-platform answer to darktable's aging OpenCL layer and the natural substrate for a vkdt-style node graph. Alternative: hand-rolled Vulkan plus MoltenVK (Apache-2.0) on macOS for maximum control. | wgpu (MIT/Apache-2.0), WGSL, MoltenVK (Apache-2.0), Dawn (BSD-3) |
| **GPU node-graph edit engine (vkdt pattern)** | must | very-high | Architect the editor as a DAG of GPU compute nodes with cached intermediates and region-of-interest evaluation, rather than darktable's fixed-order CPU pipeline — this is the decisive performance lesson from vkdt (real-time full-pipeline preview) and Ansel. It is the core engineering bet of the whole product. | vkdt design (BSD-2 portions reusable), wgpu, petgraph (Rust DAG) |
| **ML runtime via ONNX Runtime** | must | medium | MIT-licensed ONNX Runtime executes one model format across CoreML (macOS), DirectML (Windows), CUDA/TensorRT, and CPU, making it the single-integration answer for all of Lightbox's ML features. Ship models as downloadable packs to keep the installer lean, mirroring darktable 5.6's optional-AI approach. | ONNX Runtime (MIT), CoreML EP, DirectML EP, ort (Rust bindings) |
| **AI masking model stack (subject/sky/background)** | must | high | Lightroom's biggest 2020s differentiator — select subject/sky/background/people — is reproducible with Segment Anything (Apache-2.0) or its fast variants for interactive masks, U2-Net (Apache-2.0) or MODNet for salient-object/portrait matting, and simple sky segmentation models. This closes the gap no FOSS competitor has closed and should headline v1 marketing. | Segment Anything / MobileSAM (Apache-2.0), U2-Net (Apache-2.0), MODNet, ONNX Runtime |
| **Neural denoise and super-resolution models** | should | very-high | A DeepPRIME-class answer is assembled from openly licensed restoration models — NAFNet (MIT) or SCUNet for raw-domain denoising and Real-ESRGAN (BSD-3) for 2-4x enhancement — ideally run before/alongside demosaic on Bayer data, with darktable-style per-camera noise profiling to condition the model. Fine-tuning on raw bursts is the main effort. | NAFNet (MIT), SCUNet, Real-ESRGAN (BSD-3), vkdt noise-profiling approach |
| **Face detection/recognition and semantic search** | should | high | digiKam and Immich prove the recipe: YuNet detection + SFace embeddings (both Apache-2.0 via OpenCV zoo) for face clustering, and CLIP-family image/text embeddings (MIT) in a local vector index for natural-language photo search. Avoid InsightFace pretrained weights (non-commercial terms); store embeddings in SQLite with an ANN extension. | OpenCV YuNet/SFace (Apache-2.0), OpenCLIP (MIT), sqlite-vec (Apache-2.0), usearch |
| **Metadata subsystem with GPL firewall** | must | medium | EXIF/IPTC/XMP read-write is a licensing trap: exiv2 is GPL-2 (linking it contaminates a permissive app) and ExifTool is Artistic/GPL (safe only as a spawned subprocess). The clean permissive path is Adobe's BSD-licensed XMP Toolkit plus Rust crates (kamadak-exif BSD-2, quickexif) for EXIF, with ExifTool as an optional user-invoked power tool; Lightroom interop requires reading/writing the crs: develop-settings namespace and standard XMP sidecars. | Adobe XMP Toolkit SDK (BSD-3), kamadak-exif (BSD-2), ExifTool (subprocess only), XMP sidecar spec |
| **Catalog database on SQLite** | must | medium | SQLite (public domain) with WAL mode, FTS5 for text search, and a vector extension for embeddings is what Lightroom itself uses for catalogs and is the unambiguous choice; digiKam's optional-MySQL complexity and PhotoPrism's server DBs are anti-patterns for a desktop v1. Design write-ahead safety and file-relocation resilience from day one — catalog corruption is the top trust-killer cited against ON1/Luminar. | SQLite (public domain), FTS5, sqlite-vec, Litestream (backup) |
| **Non-destructive edits as sidecar/interop layer** | must | medium | Persist edit recipes in the catalog and mirror them to XMP sidecars the way darktable and ART do, ideally also parsing Lightroom's crs: parameters for one-way import — giving switchers a migration path no commercial rival offers FOSS users. Format must be versioned and forward-compatible from v1. | XMP/RDF, darktable sidecar schema (as documented reference), Adobe crs namespace docs |
| **UI framework selection for a pro imaging desktop app** | must | high | Qt (LGPL-3, dynamic-link) remains the proven choice for pro imaging (Capture One, digiKam, Krita) with mature color-managed rendering and docking; Tauri+web (MIT) or Flutter (BSD-3) give faster UI iteration but complicate zero-copy GPU texture display of a live pipeline; egui/iced (MIT) integrate best with a wgpu engine but lack widget depth; JUCE's AGPL/commercial terms make it a poor fit. Recommendation: wgpu-rendered central canvas embedded in Qt or Tauri chrome. | Qt 6 (LGPL-3), Tauri (MIT/Apache), egui/iced (MIT/Apache), Flutter (BSD-3), Electron (MIT) |
| **Tethered capture via libgphoto2** | could | medium | LGPL-2.1 libgphoto2 provides remote control and live image download for hundreds of Canon/Nikon/Sony bodies, enabling a Capture One-style tethering story that Lightroom Classic only partially matches. Vendor SDKs (Canon EDSDK, Sony SDK) can be optional closed-source plugins later. | libgphoto2 (LGPL-2.1), gphoto2-rs bindings |
| **Map/geotagging module** | could | medium | A Lightroom Map-module equivalent using MapLibre GL (BSD-3) with OpenStreetMap tiles, reverse geocoding, and drag-to-geotag — functionality digiKam and Immich both ship, so its absence would be conspicuous. Offline tile caching matters for travel workflows. | MapLibre GL (BSD-3), OpenStreetMap data (ODbL), Nominatim reverse geocoding |
| **Film emulation and creative LUT support** | could | low | Ship HaldCLUT/.cube LUT application in the pipeline and bundle the freely licensed film-simulation collections RawTherapee popularized, matching Lightroom profiles and ON1/Luminar 'looks' at near-zero engineering cost. OCIO can evaluate LUTs on GPU. | HaldCLUT collections (CC0/CC-BY), OpenColorIO, .cube parser |
| **GPL-contamination licensing policy** | must | low | A standing engineering rule that keeps Lightbox permissively licensable: MIT/BSD/Apache libraries may be linked freely; LGPL libraries (LibRaw, lensfun, libvips, Qt, libgphoto2, libheif) only via dynamic linking with relink ability; GPL/AGPL code (exiv2, darktable, RawTherapee, dcamprof, ExifTool-as-library, PhotoPrism/Immich) never linked — subprocess isolation or clean-room reimplementation only; model weights and datasets get individual license review (e.g., InsightFace weights are non-commercial). This policy is what makes the entire build-vs-buy table above coherent. | SPDX license identifiers, cargo-deny / license-checker CI tooling, REUSE spec |
